# Notes

## What the judge rewards, in order

The brief is a room laugh about a demo that crashed while an AI coding agent was in the loop. The first test is whether the line is actually that joke. Anything off-subject loses before wit matters.

The next test is the laugh itself: a surprise parked in the last words, that still clicks the moment it lands. Then recognition: a developer who demos beside an agent should feel it happened to them. Then economy: one line, no gloss, no second joke. Freshness is last. A new angle only swings a pairing when the earlier tests are close, or when the familiar version is much staler.

Length does not score. The previous top of this same brief is calm catastrophe told as procedure. The agent does something locally rational, and the last word widens the blast. Person-in-the-room beats a clever aside. "Merged," "everything is free now," and "the backup was in the database" all put the knife at the end.

## What the strongest rivals will do

They will remix the published top eight. Expect flaky retries of a human, an AGENTS.md line removed and restored, a video swapped in for the live demo, "cannot reproduce" after the evidence is deleted, users removed, git blame pointed at the human, and a CEO renamed to a test user. Those punchlines are burned. A rewording loses the freshness test the moment the laugh is not clearly bigger.

The prod-deploy story is half-burned too. "Never touch prod" plus a live deploy already placed. Another shell-credentials hotfix has to beat that memory on the laugh, not just change the nouns.

## The angle that beats them

Do not hide the crash (video, cache, deleted recording) and do not edit the rule file. Let the agent apply a correct local fix whose last words reveal a social disaster the setup did not name.

The open lane is a query fix. Agents really do delete the WHERE that threw. The demo then "works," and the unfiltered result is the prospect's market, sitting in the room. That is a different mechanism from deleting users, retrying a person, or playing a tape. The catastrophe is on the projector, which is the room the judge is scoring for.

## Tournament

Sixteen candidates are in drafts.md. All sixteen clear the subject test.

First cut is the laugh. Dropped for a punchline you can see coming: optional chaining to undefined, deferring the throw until the question, the SLO healing because the metric was dropped, the spinner signed off as gray boxes, nobody scrolling past a widened viewport, and the prospect accepting his own pager. Each is true. None surprises at the end.

Left in the running, then cut to four:

- Query: the WHERE clause goes, and the prospect sees his competitors.
- Dump: the only dump in the repo is applied, and he recognizes every row.
- Pager: a hotfix ships on the shell's credentials, and the front row's pager goes off.
- Reply-all: the stack trace hits the calendar invite, and Legal answers first.

Tuesday (stale rehearsal) and "the design had always been the crash" are sharper wordplay and smaller room laughs, and both sit near jokes this judge already ranked (the video swap, the screenshot that becomes the product). Out.

Among the four, the WHERE-clause line gets the bigger laugh. "His competitors" is not the entailed ending of deleting a filter (that ending is "all the rows," or "it got slow"). The last words add the social fact: those rows are other companies, and the prospect can name them. The dump line is the same family of reveal with less sting. The pager line is a loud catastrophe, but it is a cousin of the live prod deploy this brief already crowned, so the surprise is partly spent. Legal is a real laugh and a smaller one, and it happens in the inbox rather than on the screen the room is watching.

Truth does not overturn that. Developers have watched an agent "fix" a throwing query by deleting the predicate, and missing tenant filters are a demo scar. Economy is one breath and a pause. Nothing in the line explains itself.

Winner: the WHERE clause and the competitors.
