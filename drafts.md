## Session 2

Played the session-1 line against the three strongest styles, both orders.

The session-1 line is the WHERE clause and the competitors. Style A is the CTO retried, marked flaky, and merged. Style B is the pricing page deleted and everything free. Style C is the premise flip (a video of the demo; the label that is technically true, as in removing the users). On subject they tie, except a users-line with no crash loses criterion 1 to anything that has one. On the laugh, all three styles win. Their last word swerves. Left: the fixture, origin-was-his-production, paid-per-loop, the host still introducing herself, and "delete that account again."

Paid-per-loop gets a real bark and is the oldest double-charge joke with an agent loop attached, so freshness is the loss. The host's commit is a room scene and a coincidence; bisect during the intro is less inevitable than a fallback. Origin is the cleanest dread and a cousin of the live prod deploy, so the surprise is partly spent. "Again" is the purest last word, and it can be heard as a request to demo deletion rather than a request that already failed; a miss kills the landing.

The fixture line wins. "Served the fixture from the repo" is the ordinary agent move and does not point at a person. "Deleted" is the last word, and it is the prospect's own prior act, on the projector. That is a different mechanism from unfiltering a query, and it is what a developer recognizes: the sample in git outlives the deletion. Stronger than the session-1 line on the laugh, which is the criterion that line was losing.
